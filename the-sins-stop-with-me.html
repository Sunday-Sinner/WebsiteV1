“THE SINS STOP WITH ME”

Some cycles are passed down. That doesn't mean they have to continue.

“The Sins Stop With Me” is one of my most personal tracks. It explores addiction, family history, and the responsibility I feel for the choices ahead of me—the decision to work toward breaking unhealthy patterns instead of passing them to another generation.

01 · THE BEGINNING

WHERE THE MEANING BEGAN

This song began with something I didn't fully understand about my own family.

My dad started smoking cigarettes at a young age. He eventually stopped smoking cigarettes, and I thought he had quit nicotine altogether. I later discovered he was still dependent on nicotine.

I found this out when my dad learned that I had become hooked on nicotine myself. He decided to tell me about his own experience. My grandpa has smoked cigarettes and cigars throughout much of his life, too.

Learning that helped me see how nicotine dependence had affected multiple generations of my family. I don't think that makes my dad or my grandpa bad people. I know dependence can be difficult to overcome. I want to make different choices for my own future rather than continue the same pattern.

02 · THE OPENING · A PERSONAL NOTE

THE WEIGHT BEHIND THE WORDS

Some of the song's hardest words came from a time when I was carrying more than I knew how to explain.

BEFORE
THE MORNING

I went through a lot emotionally because of rumors at school. Friendships fell apart, and people I trusted betrayed me. It changed how I looked at friendship, trust, and the people around me.

During my sophomore year, which was last school year, I thought about suicide a lot. The opening of this song comes from how dark my thoughts could become during that time. The lyrics let me say honestly how overwhelmed and emotionally exhausted I felt, instead of pretending everything was fine.

WHEN THE NIGHT GETS TOO LOUD

“I'm sitting here with a gun to my head”

The four-in-the-morning imagery is about being alone with thoughts that are difficult to quiet. The reference to a gun communicates the severity of the emotional crisis in the song; it is not there to make that pain look beautiful or easy. These words reflect a period when I struggled. They do not define my whole identity or decide what my future has to be.

03 · BETWEEN THE LINES

WHAT THE LYRICS MEAN TO ME

A few lines hold the heart of the promise I was trying to make.

I
“Let the sins stop here
Let the cycle break
Let there be a future
That the past cannot take”

To me, these lines are about deciding that unhealthy habits don't have to keep moving into another generation.

II
“I can't change where I came from
But I can change what comes to be”

I can't rewrite my family's history. I can make choices that shape my own future.

III
“The sins of the Father
Stop here with me”

I use “sins of the Father” as a metaphor for unhealthy patterns and habits that can pass between generations. I'm not calling my family bad people. I'm saying a pattern can belong to my family's history without having to belong to my future.

These are the lines that carry the song's meaning for me.

04 · WITH COMPASSION

UNDERSTANDING MY DAD

I remembered my dad smoking cigarettes, but I believed he had quit nicotine entirely. Finding out he was still dependent changed how I understood his experience.

Quitting cigarettes and overcoming nicotine dependence are not necessarily the same thing. His experience helped me recognize how persistent addiction can be. It didn't make him a villain, and it didn't mean he had intentionally passed anything on to me.

I can understand that struggle with compassion and still choose a different direction for myself.
