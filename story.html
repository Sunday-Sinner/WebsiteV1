THE STORY

Some roads aren't chosen. You just find yourself on them.

SUNDAY SINNER

Where the road ends, another one begins.

Sunday Sinner wasn't created because everything went right.

It came from the opposite.

From growing up, losing people, being misunderstood, hearing things said about me that weren't true, and wondering how many times life could knock you off the road before you finally stopped trying to get back up.

The songs come from those feelings.

The details may change.

The names disappear.

The emotions stay.

FIRST MILE

FRESHMAN YEAR

The First Rumor

Freshman year was when I first really learned how quickly a rumor can change the way people look at you.

Something was said.

People talked.

And suddenly I was dealing with a version of myself that wasn't really me.

I tried to move past it.

I thought maybe that was the worst it would get.

I was wrong.

“I thought that was the bottom.”

THE ROAD DARKENS

SOPHOMORE YEAR

Another Rumor

Then sophomore year came around.

Another rumor.

Except this time, it felt completely different.

What happened before seemed small compared to what came next.

The new rumor felt like it was a hundred times worse.

More people knew.

More people had opinions.

And once something spreads, it can become almost impossible to explain the truth to everyone who has already decided what they believe.

That's when I started learning something:

Sometimes people don't need the truth.

They just need a story.

“When I thought life couldn't get worse, it did — another rumor, 100X worse.”

A TURN IN THE ROAD

JUNIOR YEAR

The Fight at Homecoming

By junior year, everything had already built up.

The rumors.

The friendships that changed.

The people I stopped talking to.

The feeling that no matter what I did, something else was waiting around the corner.

Then came Homecoming.

A situation turned into a fight.

And after everything that had already happened, it felt like the moment everything finally boiled over.

It wasn't just about one night.

It felt like the result of everything that had been building for years.

Afterward, I was left asking the same question I'd been asking for a long time:

How much worse can this get?

“Freshman year was a rumor. Sophomore year was another rumor — 100X worse. Junior year was a fight at Homecoming.”

Keep Driving

I don't know yet.

Maybe another road.

Maybe another chapter.

Maybe a completely different direction.

But after everything, I've learned one thing:

I don't have to know exactly where the road ends to keep driving.

REAL FEELINGS, ALTERED DETAILS

THE SONGS

These songs aren't meant to be perfect records of everything that happened.

They're stories.

Some are closer to reality than others.

Some combine different feelings into one moment.

Some turn real experiences into fictional situations.

Some are completely imagined.

But underneath all of them is something real.

The feeling.

The loneliness.

The anger.

The doubt.

The hope.

The question of whether things are happening for a reason.

The question of whether there is a way forward.

Blending gritty country, alternative rock, emo, and Southern influences, Sunday Sinner gives those feelings a raw, cinematic place to live. “Sunday” reaches toward faith, forgiveness, and hope; “Sinner” holds space for mistakes, rebellion, doubt, and questions without easy answers.

Heartbreak is not always about a single person. Sometimes it is about the collapse of a version of life you thought would hold together.

Loneliness is the feeling of being surrounded and still not feeling seen. It is a quiet ache that tends to travel well after midnight.

Faith is not always a clean certainty. Sometimes it is a prayer with no clear answer, still spoken because it helps carry the weight.

Doubt is the question that refuses to go away. It is the part of the road that asks whether the light is coming or simply the last of the day.

Growing up is learning to carry pain without letting it become the only thing you know how to feel.

Anger is sometimes a warning light. It is what shows up when you have been hurt, dismissed, or left with no good way to say it.

Hope is not optimism. It is the decision to keep moving even when the road looks empty.

Belonging is the feeling of being wanted without needing to disappear to earn it.

Leaving is not always a dramatic exit. Sometimes it is a quiet decision to stop forcing yourself to stay where you no longer fit.

Starting over can feel like driving blind, but it is also how you find out the road is wider than the last version of it.
